Human Evolution Along With AI

Thoughts on AI, humans, work, creativity, and what happens when intelligence becomes abundant.

AI is honestly one of the most exciting and scary things to be alive for right now.

Especially being a computer engineering student.

Because on one hand, everything is so much fun. I can learn something and immediately ask an AI to explain it. I can build something that would have taken me days. I can think of an idea and actually implement it. I can learn about things that I probably wouldn't have been able to learn this quickly a few years ago.

But on the other hand, sometimes I genuinely wonder what happens to us.

Like, we are computer science students. We are literally learning the things that AI is becoming increasingly good at doing.

We learn programming and AI writes code.

We learn algorithms and AI can implement them.

We learn databases and AI can write queries.

We learn machine learning and AI can help us build machine learning systems.

We learn how to build software and then we have an AI sitting there which can build a significant part of the software with us.

And then I start thinking beyond just software engineering.

What Actually Happens to Humans?

What actually happens to humans when AI becomes good enough at almost everything?

Education

Take education.

Why would I need to sit in a classroom for 50-60 minutes with 100 other students while one professor explains something at one pace?

I can sit with an AI for 50 minutes.

I can say, "I didn't understand this."

It explains again.

I can say, "Explain it like I'm five."

It does that.

Then I can say, "Now explain it mathematically."

It does that.

Then I can ask questions for another 30 minutes without feeling like I'm disturbing the class.

And the AI can know exactly what I understood and what I didn't.

A professor has to teach one class where every student has a different level of understanding.

An AI can theoretically teach every student differently.

So what exactly does the classroom become?

Maybe the classroom isn't even about learning information anymore.

Maybe it becomes about meeting people, discussions, experiments, projects, friendships, mentorship, experiences.

But then again, why do we need universities at all if knowledge is available everywhere?

  • Maybe for accreditation.
  • Maybe for research.
  • Maybe for social development.
  • Maybe for things we haven't even figured out yet.

Medicine

And then I think about doctors.

Today if I have symptoms, I go to a doctor.

But I can already ask an AI about my symptoms and get possible explanations, questions I should ask, things I should be worried about, medicines that are commonly used, what tests might be relevant, etc.

Obviously there are huge limitations and risks, and I am not saying an AI should replace a doctor.

But the interesting part is that the information that was once locked inside the head of a professional is becoming accessible to almost everyone.

And then there is another weird thought.

Doctors sometimes change medicines if the first one doesn't work.

Obviously that isn't simply random experimentation — there is medical reasoning, clinical evidence, monitoring and responsibility involved.

But from the outside, you start wondering:

If an AI has access to millions of medical papers, clinical guidelines, patient histories and outcomes, how much of that decision-making can eventually be assisted or even automated?

And then robotics enters the picture.

Robots can already perform extremely precise movements.

So what happens when robots become extremely good at surgery?

Does the doctor disappear?

Or does the doctor become the person supervising the entire system?

Law, Business and Professional Work

And then lawyers.

AI can read thousands of pages of documents much faster than a human.

It can search laws and previous cases.

It can draft contracts.

It can summarize huge amounts of information.

So if the information itself is no longer scarce, what exactly are lawyers spending most of their time doing?

Then accountants.

Then HR.

Then customer support.

Then analysts.

Then consultants.

Then managers.

Then designers.

Then architects.

Then engineers.

Then researchers.

What Happens to Artists?

And then I start thinking about artists.

AI can already create paintings, illustrations, music, videos, voices, characters, animations.

And people are actually consuming this stuff.

So if I can describe an image and get it in seconds, what happens to the value of the ability to physically draw that image?

If I can describe a song and get a song, what happens to music?

If I can describe a movie and get a movie, what happens to filmmaking?

If I can create a virtual person that looks, sounds and behaves like a real person, what happens to actors and influencers?

Content Creation

And content creation becomes even crazier.

One person with AI could potentially do what previously required an entire team.

  • Writing.
  • Editing.
  • Thumbnail.
  • Video.
  • Voice.
  • Animation.
  • Research.
  • Marketing.

Maybe one person becomes an entire media company.

Physical Work

And then there is physical work.

  • House help.
  • Cleaning.
  • Cooking.
  • Laundry.
  • Delivery.
  • Driving.
  • Construction.
  • Factories.

If robots become good enough, even physical labour starts becoming automated.

We already have robotic vacuum cleaners that can clean a house without anyone doing anything.

Imagine when robots become capable of doing much more than vacuuming.

Then what happens to all the ordinary work humans currently do simply because someone has to do it?

What Exactly Is Left for Humans?

And then I get to the really weird question.

If AI can do intellectual work and robots can do physical work...

what exactly is left for humans?

And I don't mean this in a depressing way.

Actually, I find it fascinating.

Because maybe we have been defining human value based on the wrong things.

For most of human history, being useful meant being able to do something.

  • Being physically strong.
  • Being able to calculate.
  • Being able to remember.
  • Being able to write.
  • Being able to build.
  • Being able to analyze.
  • Being able to create.

And now machines are becoming better at many of those things.

So maybe the definition of "useful" itself changes.

Maybe humans become more focused on deciding what should be done rather than how it should be done.

Maybe the important skill becomes knowing what question to ask.

Maybe creativity becomes less about manually producing something and more about having something meaningful to say.

Maybe learning becomes less about memorizing information and more about understanding, questioning and connecting ideas.

Maybe a computer science student doesn't need to compete with AI at writing code.

Maybe they need to understand computers deeply enough to tell the AI what system should exist in the first place.

But What If AI Can Do That Too?

But then I also wonder...

What if AI becomes good at that too?

What if it can decide what should be built?

What if it can conduct research?

What if it can discover new medicines?

What if it can design better AI?

What if it can improve itself?

What if eventually almost everything that we currently consider "work" can be done by machines?

Then the Problem Becomes Meaning

Then the problem isn't really unemployment anymore.

The problem becomes meaning.

Because if I don't have to work to survive, what do I do?

If I don't need to study to get a job, why do I study?

If I don't need to cook, clean, drive, code, calculate, research or write, what do I spend my day doing?

  • Maybe people will create.
  • Maybe people will explore.
  • Maybe people will make art.
  • Maybe people will travel.
  • Maybe people will spend more time with family.
  • Maybe people will play games.
  • Maybe people will study simply because they are curious.
  • Maybe we will explore space.
  • Maybe we will spend more time understanding ourselves.

Or maybe humans will just find new things to compete over.

Because even if machines make everything abundant, humans may still want status, attention, money, power and recognition.

And that's another thing that scares me.

AI Doesn't Automatically Create a Better World

AI might not automatically create a better world.

It could make people incredibly powerful.

And powerful tools can be used in completely different ways.

So I don't think the future is simply going to be:

AI replaces humans.

It might instead be:

AI changes what being human means.

Being a Computer Science Student Right Now

And maybe that is why being a computer science student right now feels so strange.

We are learning these technologies at the exact moment they are beginning to change the meaning of the skills we are learning.

We are not just learning how to use computers anymore.

We are learning how to coexist with systems that can increasingly reason, create, teach, code, research and eventually act in the physical world.

And honestly, I don't know where this ends.

Maybe that's the most interesting part.

I don't think anyone really knows.

What Could the Future Look Like?

Maybe 20 years from now, some of the jobs we think are completely safe will look completely different.

Maybe some jobs we think will disappear will survive for reasons we can't currently predict.

Maybe schools will still exist, but for completely different reasons.

Maybe doctors will still exist, but AI will be sitting beside every doctor.

Maybe programmers will still exist, but writing code will be a tiny part of programming.

Maybe artists will still exist, but the definition of an artist will change.

Maybe humans will still work even when they don't technically have to.

Or maybe the biggest change won't be about jobs at all.

The Scarcity of Intelligence

Maybe one day we'll look back at this period and realize that AI wasn't really about replacing individual professions.

It was about removing the scarcity of intelligence.

And if intelligence becomes abundant...

then the question changes from:

"What can humans do?"

to:

"What do humans want to do?"

Final Thoughts

And I genuinely don't know what the answer to that is.
